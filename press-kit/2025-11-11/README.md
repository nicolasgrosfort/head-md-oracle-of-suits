# Anachrony

Being a Wild Card

## Pitch

### Short

Anachrony is a contemplative interactive installation where players travel through four key eras in the history of playing cards. Using hand gestures, they navigate minimalist digital landscapes to recover misplaced cards scattered across time. Each recovered card restores order to its world—until the player discovers that the chaos was caused by the Joker, a "wild card" who, tired of being no one and everyone at once, has tried to steal the identity of other cards… and finally, of the player themself.

### Long

Anachrony is a contemplative interactive installation set inside a spacecraft, where the player sits at the helm and observes time unfold across distant worlds. Through hand-tracking interactions, the player navigates four key eras in the history of playing cards — each represented as a minimalist digital landscape.

In these worlds, cards have slipped out of their time, drifting like fragments of memory. By moving their hands, the player scans the environment to locate and restore these wandering cards to their rightful era. Each discovery reveals a short story or fun fact about the card's cultural origin, turning exploration into a learning experience as well as a visual journey.

As more cards are recovered, the fractured timelines begin to realign, yet something feels off. In the final sequence, the player learns that the temporal disorder was caused by the Joker — a "wild card" tired of being undefined, trying to steal the identity of others… before ultimately taking the player’s own.

## Story board

### 0-SBY

The installation rests in standby mode. When the player approaches and sits in front of the screen, the system recognizes their presence and awakens. The spacecraft lights up, inviting them to begin.

#### 1-VSL

Seated at the ship’s controls, the player turns a virtual dial with their hand to travel between worlds. After each journey, they return to the cockpit, where the recovered cards appear on the control table. Subtle clues hint that the ship belongs to the Joker.

### World sequences

Across the following worlds, the player repeats the same contemplative interaction — using hand gestures to find and collect misplaced cards. Some are blank, others in their proper era, a few out of place. Each discovery reveals a glimpse of its origin before the player returns to the ship.

#### 2-MMK

The player is in a Mamluk desert. Cards drift slowly through the sky like fragments of time.

#### 3-ACN

The player stands before an ancient Chinese temple. Cards float on calm water, reflecting the passing time.

#### 4-EMD

The player is inside a medieval European castle. Cards fall gently from the ceiling onto a large wooden table.

#### 5-EJP

To be designed.

### 6-JKR

Back in the ship, the player encounters the Joker, materialized as shifting ASCII characters — a digital reflection of the machine and of themselves. With a hand gesture, they dissolve the characters, breaking free. The cockpit lights fade to black. When a new visitor sits down, the system awakens once again — the loop begins anew.

## Field observations

### Journey

My research began with the materiality of playing cards — their texture, manufacturing processes, and physical properties. This led me to study the objects and gestures of cheating, as a way to understand how material and manipulation intertwine.

From there, I explored the mirror ("shiner") as an interface — a poetic object that reveals what is usually invisible. The mirror later gave way to the story of Bianco's 19th-century scam, where cards became tools of deception and illusion.

When this narrative became too narrow, it coincided with the moment we formed groups and began merging ideas. The project quickly evolved into a collection of disparate mini-games, closer to WarioWare than to a cohesive narrative experience. This lack of depth led me to step back and rebuild the conceptual framework.

After our group split up, I decided to continue the project on my own. I based my installation on the card-collecting mechanic and the story of the Joker. I designed a flexible system able to cover different eras and cultures related to playing cards, connected through a main narrative inspired by the Joker’s dual nature. The installation also shares short anecdotes while displaying various card designs.

### Insights

#### Story of the Jocker

The Joker is a wild card — one that can represent any other.
He "teaches people not to take life too seriously" and "sees the whole world as a game."
Linked to Le Mat (The Fool) in tarot, he embodies both the beginning and the end of a journey.

- [Joker](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joker_(playing_card)>)
- [The Joker Card](https://www.wopc.co.uk/playing-cards/the-joker-card)
- [Le Mat](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mat)

#### Collection as central mechanic

Inspired by Pokémon and the Pokédex, the project uses collection as a way to tell stories through discovery and repair — a kind of bestiary of playing cards. This mechanic recalls early collectible cards hidden in everyday products like cigarette packs.

- [Trading Playing Cards](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/trading-playing-cards)
- [Pokedex](https://www.pokepedia.fr/Pokédex)
- [Bestiaire de l'anthropocène](https://artfiction.ch/produit/bestiaire-de-lanthropocene/)

#### Creative coding workshop

The creative coding workshop served to test gestures as storytelling tools and explore possibilities with Media Pipe library. The interaction was refined to a single hand movement — holding to confirm — emphasizing time and intention.
The main goal was to make onboarding optional, where the gesture itself provides sufficient affordance.

- [The Chronoscope](https://github.com/nicolasgrosfort/head-md-oracle-of-suits/blob/main/process/gestures/the-chronoscope.md)
- [Chronoscope lamp](https://github.com/nicolasgrosfort/head-md-oracle-of-suits/tree/main/code/2025-10-24/chronoscope-lamp)
- [3D zoom](https://github.com/nicolasgrosfort/head-md-oracle-of-suits/tree/main/code/2025-10-28/3d-zoom)

#### Visuals inspirations

The wooden screen installation combines the aesthetics of playing card shapes, the Pokédex, and the fragility of a card castle.

The visual style blends cartoon and early video-game influences — somewhere between a children’s illustration and a soft-edged 8-bit interface. This simple, playful design leaves space for the imagination while keeping the focus on the cards themselves, which serve as the main visual and narrative elements of the experience.

Each world reinterprets landscapes from different eras and cultures through minimalist 2D environments.

Since the project was restarted from scratch on 2025 October 17 , the user tests conducted before that date are no longer relevant.

### 2025-11-06

Tests were conducted in class with participants who had never seen the project before.

**To test**
